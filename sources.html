The research

What sits behind each world

I’m a mom, not a scientist, so I hold myself to a few simple rules.

  • Real and findablePeer-reviewed studies, major reviews, and official guidelines. Each link goes to the original.
  • Linked to the sourceMost links are DOIs, permanent addresses that point to the official version of a paper.
  • Honest about limitsWhen a study shows a link, not cause and effect, I say so.
  • Plain wordsI translate the findings, but I don’t stretch them.

Across the worlds: places and policy

Most sources here look at what happens in a family. This one zooms out to the neighborhood, the city, and the laws that shape them. It is written to guide policymakers, which is a reminder that a child’s world is also built by the choices communities make together.

Books for grown-ups

On my nightstand

The books that shaped how I think about food, the gut, and nature, plus more good reads for curious parents.

My shelf

This Is Your Brain on FoodUma Naidoo, MD · 2020

A Harvard nutritional psychiatrist on how what we eat shapes mood, focus, and the gut–brain connection.

The Mind-Gut ConnectionEmeran Mayer, MD · 2016

A gastroenterologist and neuroscientist on the constant conversation between the gut, its microbes, and the brain.

Alchemy of HerbsRosalee de la Forêt · 2017

How everyday kitchen herbs and spices work, with simple recipes. A lovely companion to our spice adventures.

More good reads

Until the Streetlights Come OnGinny Yurich · 2023

The founder of 1000 Hours Outside on bringing back unstructured play.

Recent
Hunt, Gather, ParentMichaeleen Doucleff · 2021

A science journalist travels with her daughter to learn how families in other cultures raise helpful, confident kids.

Recent
Let Them Eat DirtB. Brett Finlay & Marie-Claire Arrieta · 2016

Two microbiologists on children’s microbiomes, written for parents.

Last Child in the WoodsRichard Louv · 2005

The book that started the modern conversation about children and nature.

There’s No Such Thing as Bad WeatherLinda Åkeson McGurk · 2017

A Swedish-American mom on Scandinavian outdoor childhood.

A pediatric occupational therapist on why kids need free, outdoor movement.

A developmental psychologist on why raising children is more like tending a garden than following a plan.

A short, beautiful essay about exploring nature with a child.
